vast 55981105 (~$0.8/h), image nvidia/cuda:12.6.3-devel, ptxas pinned 12.6.77 via pip wheel. Code: bench_real/kernl_fused_ff/
tools/probe_loop.py (binary SASS patch), make_ff_probes.py, probe_analysis_ff.py; harness record 48 -> 64 B.

METHOD: per probe position k one trace cubin with `CS2R` at the loop top and `CS2R` after body instruction k (stall 1,
no scoreboard), stored by the exit stamp callee at record + 48 (callee return addresses = MOV R10 immediates, patched;
BRA/BSSY/CALL/RET 50-bit offsets relocated; verified by re-disassembly). Values are from the LAST iteration of the K loop
(the registers are overwritten every iteration and read at exit). probe - loop_top = issue cycle of instruction k in that
iteration. Median over 256 warps (128 CTAs x 2 warps; ~1.2 CTAs/SM) and 3 launches. Step 3 (every third instruction).

READING: in the last iteration ro and tt38 are equal (643 vs 673 cy) and orig is 1.6-1.9x slower, matching the 1.6x whole-kernel
speed-up of ro over orig at this shape. tt38 vs ro: Triton 3.8 pays ~130 cycles at the first consumer of the x tile that it
routes through shared memory (cp.async -> LDS.128 -> HMUL2); our register prefetch does not pay it, and spends it elsewhere
(longer body, 270 vs 215 instructions). Whole-kernel they tie (0.99x).

CAVEAT (important): last-iteration values. In a software-pipelined loop the last iteration's cp.async data were issued
iterations earlier, so DEPBAR waits for DRAM/L2 are mostly absent in it; steady-state iterations at the decode shape wait more
(measured 1295 cy/iter mean vs 673 here). Next version: accumulate (probe - loop_top) over all iterations (2 x IADD3 per
iteration) and divide by the iteration count -> mean issue time per instruction across the loop. The orig variant's exposed
LDG shows in every iteration, so its 240-310-cycle wait is representative as is.
