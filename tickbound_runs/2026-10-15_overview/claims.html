5Measured inside CuAsmRL's own action space, with sound timing, the gains are at most 2.2%. Their reported 2 to 26% is launch noise.

1.08-1.33xCuAsmRL's timing method on UNCHANGED kernels

Their action space = move one memory instruction one slot inside a basic block. We enumerated every legal move (window 12, CuAssembler + our legality checker, byte-identical outputs on three input sets) and timed with CUDA graphs and paired sign tests. Their method (CUDA events over back-to-back launches, Triton 2.1) is launch-bound, so it reports speed-ups for kernels that did not change. We did not run their RL search head-to-head on the same GPU (3.6 h per kernel).

6Limits, stated.

  • Reordering removes exposed loads. Where a kernel has none (Triton 3.8 at 64x32x32, issue-bound), it ties; where a deeper cp.async pipeline exists (3.8 at 32x64x32, cold, large K), it loses.
  • The automatic Triton-corpus pass (Liger, FlagGems, CuAsmRL; ~100 kernels, Triton 3.2 to 3.8) has median 1.00: Triton 3.x already pipelines tl.dot loops; wins are limited to RMSNorm-shaped loops (up to 1.43x).
  • Larger wins from changing num_warps / num_stages exist (3.31x on fused FF decode) and are excluded from every claim above because they are not reordering.
  • Open: the Triton 2.2 original at 16x16x64 gives 995 cy/iter accumulated vs 1076 last-iteration vs 2253 lifetime (2.3x disagreement, unexplained). H100 reruns of the fixed traces and both probes are pending. Nothing from 13 to 14 Oct is committed.
