Speed-up per kernel (hand reorder, and the automatic pass where it ran)

Link 1: region traces, Kernl RMSNorm

37 cases x 4 variants, 132 per-warp traces. Pass 1 (load x, sum of squares) is unchanged by every reorder; the whole gain is in pass 2 (load rms_w, normalise, store). Pass-2 share of the saving: median 1.02 across 111 pairs. Pre-registered predictions hit 78/78 at M ≤ 64, 92/111 overall.

Link 2 and 3: sliding probe, Kernl RMSNorm pass 2

A100, N4096 M16 warm L2, one build per probe position (172 positions x 4 variants). Flat = issuing one instruction per few cycles. A vertical jump = the warp waiting for a load at its first consumer. orig has four jumps of 192-226 cycles at the four HADD2.F32 consumers (an L2 round trip each); ro removes two, ro2 and ro3 all four. Cycles accounted by the jumps vs cycles removed by the stopwatch: 636 vs 639, 1031 vs 1026, 1077 vs 1086. Probe cost ≤ 4 cycles.

Link 2 and 3: loop probe, Kernl fused FF at the decode shape vs Triton 3.8

M16 N4096 K4096, config 16x16x64 w4 s2, mean over all 64 K-loop iterations (accumulated in a register), median over 1024 warps and 3 launches. Triton 3.8 waits 732 cycles at DEPBAR.LE SB0 on every iteration: its cp.async pipeline is 2 stages deep and the x tile comes from DRAM. Our register prefetch on Triton 2.2 waits once, 730 cycles at the SEL where the prefetched tile lands, and finishes the iteration in 1430 vs 2463 cycles; whole kernel 83 vs 158 us. The Triton 2.2 original is left off this chart: its accumulated value undercounts 2.3x against the last-iteration and lifetime checks and is unexplained.

Same loop, config 64x32x32: where Triton 3.8 wins

Triton 3.8 is issue-bound here (670 of a 710-cycle lifetime bound); nothing is left to schedule, and our reorder ties it (957 at decode is one +315 wait at the prefetch landing; 678 at M512). The Triton 2.2 original pays 643 cycles at the HMUL2 that first uses its in-loop load. This is the honest limit: reordering removes exposed loads; it cannot beat a kernel that has none.
